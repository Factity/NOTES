
One of my favorite short stories written on the topic of ai safety is The King and the Golem. It is a phenomenal work by Richard Ngo. The king and his mighty mission to search for the most trusted thing in the kingdom. Spoilers ahead if you havent read the work please check it out before you read further I highly recommend it you can find true brilliance and genuine intellect in his work. So the story goes like A very  hypervigilant king wanted to know if there is such a thing that he could trust unconditionally to find this he sent a message across all of his kingdom far and wide. To answer his calls different people from various backgrounds approach his court. A merchant with a sick begger, A mother with a quiet child, A philosopher with a mirror, A economist with a textbook, A farmer with a plough etc. Each of them state that the people or the things they bought were the most trusted things. 

It doesnt take very long for the smart and the mighty king to see through there flaws and point it to the people involved. The way Richard presents it is very witty. Weary of the search and unable to find a solution to this. The king decided to build his own version of a machine and commissions to build a golem with his wised of scholars writing scrolls of instructions for the machines. 

As the time passed the golem became better and better at almost all the tasks from teacher to a tax man it can do it all and with its help the kingdom flourishes. Evene with all his best efforts the king was unable to sway the loyalty and the trust of the golem. One fine day jhaving given up on the his search to understand the intent of the golem he calls for it to be summoned and asks


can I trust you enough to let you remain the right hand of the crown?”

to which the golem simply answers 

“Of course, sire,” it responded. “I was designed, built and raised to be trustworthy. I have made mistakes, but none from perfidy or malice.”

with a couple of conversations later having given up on his quest to find the true intent the wise king simply lays down his crown and the day is lost to twilight. 


the story touches on some of the deeper aspects of human ingenuity, honesty, values, morality, dependency, independence etc. the parallels the author draws between the golem and the AI is simply brilliant. 

Even if we build the super intelligence are we sure it is aligned is it a never ending battle of distrust that we have to live with. it also touches on the aspect of intent vs action. How much pursion was necessary. It is wise to expect an truly loyal golem which is trained by species that literraly kills its own kind in the name of race, religion creed. Are human values the right ones etc. World has been the most peaceful that it ever was since the dawn of time. it is hard to imagine if I was born 80 years ago I would have been a slave under the colonial rule. The amount of wealth inequality lack of opportunity and the never ending greed of the human race. Are we truly the right beings to instill moral values in the artificial intelligence. Arent we going to be similar to ants for such a being. Even if we manage it to align it with our needs How do we secure it ensure such a loyal thing exists. To expect loyalty when we as sepcies are so obivously morally flawed is such a aim less taget to shoot for.
